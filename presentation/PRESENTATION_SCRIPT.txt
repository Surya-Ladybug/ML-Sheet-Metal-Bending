  PRESENTATION SCRIPT - DETAILED TALKING POINTS
  What to Say for Each Slide
═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

[SLIDE 1: Title - 30 seconds]
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What to Say:
  "Good morning/afternoon professors and fellow students. Today
  we present an ML-assisted digital twin for sheet metal bending
  operations. Our project addresses a real industry problem:
  the computational cost of finite element simulations."

Key Points to Emphasize:
  - Real industry application
  - FEM is accurate but slow
  - ML provides practical alternative

Transition:
  "Let me start by explaining the problem we're solving..."

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SLIDE 2: Problem - 45 seconds]
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What to Say:
  "Sheet metal V-bending is a fundamental manufacturing process.
  To optimize tool parameters, engineers typically use LS-DYNA,
  a finite element method software. However, each simulation
  takes 2-4 hours to complete. For design optimization, which
  requires testing hundreds of parameter combinations, this
  becomes completely impractical - taking days or even weeks."

Key Points:
  - LS-DYNA is industry standard
  - 2-4 hours per simulation
  - Design iteration is the bottleneck
  - Days/weeks total development time

Transition:
  "This is where machine learning can help..."

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SLIDE 3: Solution - 45 seconds]
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What to Say:
  "Our solution uses machine learning surrogates. We train ML
  models on a small dataset of 16 FEM simulations - this is a
  one-time cost. Once trained, the model can predict outcomes
  in under 100 milliseconds. That's a speed improvement of more
  than 10,000 times compared to FEM."

Key Points:
  - One-time training cost
  - 16 simulations sufficient
  - < 100ms prediction time
  - 10,000x faster than FEM

Transition:
  "The key innovation is our two-model architecture..."

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SLIDE 4: Architecture - 60 seconds]
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What to Say:
  "We use TWO separate ML models because they solve different
  mathematical problems. Model 1, the Inverse Regressor, takes
  the target angle and RX constraint as inputs and predicts the
  required RYR parameter and expected inner radius. This is a
  GradientBoosting model.
  
  Model 2, the Forward Predictor, takes the actual tool
  parameters - RX and RYR - and predicts what stable angle
  we'll actually get after tool release. This uses RandomForest.
  
  Together, these models provide everything needed for robot
  programming."

Key Points:
  - Two models solve different problems
  - Inverse: design tool (what inputs to use)
  - Forward: prediction (what output to expect)
  - Different algorithms for different tasks

Transition:
  "Let me explain our training data..."

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SLIDE 5: Dataset - 45 seconds]
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What to Say:
  "Our training dataset consists of 16 LS-DYNA simulations for
  stainless steel with 3mm thickness. The parameters were
  sampled across the practical range: RX from -10 to -140
  degrees, and RYR from 0.3 to 1.8. While 16 samples may seem
  small, it's sufficient because we're using ensemble methods
  and the parameter space is well-defined."

Key Points:
  - 16 FEM simulations
  - Stainless steel, 3mm
  - Well-sampled parameter space
  - Sufficient for ensemble methods

Transition:
  "Let's look at the forward model first..."

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SLIDE 6: Model 1 - 60 seconds]
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What to Say:
  "The forward prediction model uses RandomForest with 100
  trees. It takes RX and RYR as inputs and predicts the stable
  part angle after tool release. Our test accuracy shows a Mean
  Absolute Error of 9.4 degrees with an R-squared of 0.72.
  
  Feature importance analysis reveals that RYR is the dominant
  factor, accounting for 79% of the prediction power, while RX
  contributes 21%. We chose RandomForest because it captures
  complex, non-linear material behavior without requiring
  physics-based constitutive equations."

Key Points:
  - RandomForest (100 trees)
  - Test MAE: 9.4°
  - RYR dominates (79%)
  - No physics assumptions needed

Transition:
  "Now for the more challenging inverse model..."

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SLIDE 7: Model 2 - 60 seconds]
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What to Say:
  "The inverse model is more challenging because we're solving
  what's called an ill-posed problem. Given a target angle and
  RX constraint, we need to predict the required RYR and inner
  radius. Why is this hard? Because the forward relationship is
  non-unique - multiple input combinations can produce similar
  outputs. Also, material behavior is path-dependent.
  
  We use GradientBoosting as a multi-output regressor. The
  results are quite good: RYR prediction has a Mean Absolute
  Error of only 0.30, and radius prediction has an MAE of
  4.6 millimeters."

Key Points:
  - Inverse problem is mathematically hard
  - Non-unique (multiple solutions possible)
  - GradientBoosting multi-output
  - Good accuracy despite complexity

Transition:
  "We wrapped this in a professional user interface..."

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SLIDE 8: Interface - 45 seconds]
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What to Say:
  "We built a production-ready web interface using Streamlit.
  The key design principle was transparency - all formulas are
  visible, all calculations are shown step-by-step. This meets
  academic review standards and helps users understand the
  predictions. The interface is clean, text-input only, with
  real-time predictions and CSV export for robot programming."

Key Points:
  - Streamlit framework
  - All formulas visible
  - Step-by-step transparency
  - Production-ready

Transition:
  "Let's look at the performance metrics..."

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SLIDE 9: Results - 60 seconds]
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What to Say:
  "The performance comparison is striking. LS-DYNA FEM takes 2
  to 4 hours per simulation. Our ML surrogate produces results
  in under 100 milliseconds. That's a speedup of 72,000 to
  144,000 times. The accuracy is reasonable - 9.4 degree MAE
  for angle prediction is acceptable for initial design
  iteration. Critical designs can still be validated with FEM
  after ML-guided optimization narrows the parameter space."

Key Points:
  - 72,000x - 144,000x speedup
  - Reasonable accuracy
  - Suitable for design iteration
  - Can validate critical cases with FEM

Transition:
  "Let me show you the tool in action..."

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
[SLIDE 10: Demo - 90 seconds]
──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

What to Say:
  "Here's a live demonstration. [Open http://localhost:8501]
  
  I'll set a target angle of 45 degrees and an RX constraint
  of -20 degrees. When I click Compute, you see instant results.
  The tool predicts an RYR of 1.0, an inner radius of 9.15mm,
  and shows that we need to command the tool to 38.76 degrees
  to achieve our target after material relaxation.
  
  Notice how all calculations are visible - the formulas,
  the step-by-step pipeline, everything is transparent. The
  visualizations show the bending profile and angle comparison.
  At the bottom, we can export the complete robot toolpath as
  a CSV file."

Key Points:
  - Live demonstration
  - Instant results
  - Transparent calculations
  - Export ready for robots

Transition:
  "To summarize our achievements..."

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

Key Points:
  - 10,000x speed improvement
